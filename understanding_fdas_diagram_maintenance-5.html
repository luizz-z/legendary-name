A simple learning guide to the Fire Detection and Alarm System (FDAS), its control panel, wiring concepts, and proper maintenance.

1. What is FDAS?

A Fire Detection and Alarm System (FDAS) detects signs of fire and alerts building occupants. The Fire Alarm Control Panel (FACP) is the central unit that receives signals from initiating devices and manages alarm notifications.

Detectors send signals to the control panel. When an alarm condition is identified, the panel activates notification devices such as horns, bells, or strobes.

Types of Fire Alarm Control Panels

Conventional panel

Groups devices into zones. The panel identifies the zone where an alarm or fault occurs, rather than the exact individual device.

Addressable panel

Gives each connected device a unique address, helping identify the specific device reporting an alarm or fault.

Hybrid panel

Combines conventional and addressable technologies.

2. Important Components and Wiring Concepts

FACP

The central control unit that monitors the system and manages alarm signals.

Initiating devices

Devices such as smoke detectors, heat detectors, and manual call points that report fire-related conditions.

Notification devices

Audible or visible devices that warn occupants, such as bells, horns, and strobes.

EOL resistor

An End-of-Line resistor used by a supervised circuit so the panel can monitor wiring integrity. Its value and placement must follow the panel manufacturer's instructions.

Repeater panel

A remote display that mirrors information from the main fire alarm control panel at another location.

Wiring

Connects field devices and notification appliances to the control panel. Follow the approved wiring diagram and manufacturer requirements.

3. FDAS Maintenance

Regular inspection and maintenance help keep the fire alarm system reliable and ready to operate.

Maintenance checklist

  • Inspect the control panel for trouble indicators, damage, and abnormal conditions.
  • Check detectors and keep them clean and unobstructed.
  • Inspect backup batteries and their connections.
  • Test notification devices to confirm they operate correctly.
  • Review wiring, labels, and accessible connections for visible damage.
  • Record inspections, tests, faults, and corrective actions in the maintenance documentation.

Common maintenance activities

  1. Inspection: Look for damage, dust, loose connections, and trouble signals.
  2. Cleaning: Clean detectors and equipment according to manufacturer instructions.
  3. Testing: Test devices and alarm functions using approved procedures.
  4. System checkup: Confirm that the panel, devices, batteries, and notification appliances work together.
  5. Component replacement: Replace faulty or expired parts with compatible approved components.
Safety reminder: FDAS testing and maintenance should be performed by qualified personnel, following the manufacturer's instructions, approved plans, and applicable fire-safety requirements. Do not disable or disconnect a working alarm system without an authorized procedure.
